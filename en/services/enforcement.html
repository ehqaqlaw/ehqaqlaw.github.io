FAQ

Before you request

What is an enforceable instrument?

A document that the law allows to be enforced directly through the enforcement court without a substantive lawsuit. It includes final judgments, commercial instruments such as cheques and promissory notes, notarised contracts and documents, and leases registered on the approved platform, subject to the conditions set by the Enforcement Law.

How long does enforcement take?

There is no fixed period; it depends on the debtor's response, the assets they visibly hold, and the measures taken. We tell you what to expect at each stage, and we guarantee neither a timeline nor collection.

What if the debtor has no visible assets?

The enforcement court takes the disclosure and attachment measures provided for by law, and the debtor may face further statutory measures. We tell you what can and cannot be done; a settlement or rescheduling may be the practical option.

Do I need a judgment before enforcing a cheque?

Generally, no. Cheques and promissory notes are instruments that can be enforced directly, subject to conditions such as the completeness of the instrument and the right not having lapsed. We assess the instrument first and confirm.

Are your fees a percentage of the amount collected?

Fees are set in the proposal according to the size of the file and its stages, and may be a fixed amount per stage or another formula agreed in writing. We always state what the proposal covers and what it does not.
