The Commercial Lease: What to Review Before You Sign

A shop, an office or a warehouse: the contract you sign today decides what happens at renewal, on late payment, and on moving out. Eleven clauses to read before you sign and register.

Reviewed by the Content Editorial Department · Last updated: 18 September 2026 · Related service: Real Estate & Leasing

The commercial lease is among the contracts most often signed in a hurry, because both parties are thinking about the premises rather than the paper. It is also among the contracts that most often come back to the table: at renewal, at a rent increase, on late payment, and on moving out. These are the clauses we recommend reading carefully before signing, whether you are the landlord or the tenant.

1. The parties and their capacity

Make sure the person signing as landlord is in fact the owner under the title deed, or their agent under a valid power of attorney authorising letting. For a corporate tenant: the commercial register and who represents it. A contract signed by someone without capacity is a contract waiting for a dispute.

2. The premises and their condition on handover

A precise description of the premises: location, area, facilities, and the meters and their readings. Most important of all: a handover report with photographs recording the condition on receipt, because it is the only reference at the end of the lease when the parties disagree about what was there and what was damaged.

3. Term, renewal and vacating

Does the lease renew automatically or does it require a new agreement? What is the notice period for non-renewal and who gives it? What is the period for vacating after the term ends? Silence on these questions leaves the parties facing each other at the end of every year.

4. Rent, increases and payment

The amount of rent, payment dates and method, and the effect of late payment. If there is an increase on renewal, set its rate or its calculation mechanism in advance; an increase “according to the market” is a formula for dispute, not agreement.

5. Permitted use and licences

Specify the permitted activity, who is responsible for the premises complying with the authorities' requirements for that activity, and who obtains the licences. A tenant who discovers after signing that the premises are not lawfully suitable for their activity faces two options, both costly.

6. Maintenance

The usual position is that structural and major maintenance falls on the landlord and operational maintenance on the tenant, but “major” and “operational” are elastic words. Define them with examples: central air conditioning, the main electrical supply, plumbing, facades, elevators, and who bears each.

7. Improvements and fit-out

A commercial tenant spends on fit-out and decoration, so who owns these improvements when the lease ends? Is the tenant required to remove them and reinstate the premises, or do they remain with the landlord, and is the tenant compensated? Settling this clause in advance saves a certain dispute on moving out.

8. Subletting and assignment

May the tenant sublet part of the premises or assign the lease when selling the business? On what conditions? An absolute prohibition may block the sale of the business, and an absolute permission may bring the landlord a tenant they do not know.

9. Early termination

What happens if the property is sold, the landlord decides to demolish or redevelop it, or the tenant needs to leave before the term? Set the events, the notice and the compensation for each, rather than leaving them to later assessment.

10. The security deposit

Its amount, what may be deducted from it, when it is returned after vacating, and what proves a deduction. Tie it to the handover and return reports.

11. Registration on Ejar and its effect

Registering the contract on the Ejar electronic leasing network gives it enforceable effect within the scope of the contract's terms and as the law provides, so that it can be submitted to the enforcement court for rent or eviction claims according to its clauses. This means every clause you sign will be enforced as written, so read it before registration, not after.

Checklist

  1. The signatories' capacity: the deed or power of attorney, and the commercial register.
  2. Description of the premises, a photographic handover report, and meter readings.
  3. Term, renewal, notice and the vacating period.
  4. Rent, its dates, and the increase mechanism.
  5. Permitted activity and responsibility for licences.
  6. Allocation of maintenance with specific examples.
  7. The fate of improvements on moving out.
  8. Subletting and assignment.
  9. Early termination events and their consequences.
  10. The security deposit and the conditions for its return.
  11. Registration on Ejar, and checking that the registered contract matches what was agreed.
